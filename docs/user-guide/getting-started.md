# Getting started

Atlas of Art lets you wander through art from museums around the world, arranged by **where** it was made, **when** it was made and **in what style**.

## What you can do

| Feature | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Journeys | Home page | One click opens a curated tour, such as "Ukiyo-e in Edo Japan" or "Arts of West & Central Africa" |
| Browse shortcuts | Home page | Pick a region on the map, a century or a style |
| Explore | **Explore** in the menu | Combine region, era, decade, style and keyword filters |
| Three views | Explore | A grid of images, a timeline by decade or century, or a map by region |
| Artwork pages | Click any artwork | Large image with zoom, details, licence, a link to the museum, related works |
| Favourites | Heart icon | Save works in your browser; export and import them |
| Shareable links | Address bar | Every filter combination is a link you can send to someone |
| Light and dark mode | Sun or moon icon | Switch themes; by default the site follows your device |

## Your first five minutes

1. On the **home page**, choose a **Journey** card, such as *Romanticism across Europe*.
2. Explore opens with that journey's filters already set. You'll see them as pills above the results.
3. Scroll down. More works load automatically from several museums.
4. Open the **Era** section and pick a decade, such as *1810s*, to narrow the results.
5. Click a painting you like, then the **heart** to save it.
6. Open **Favourites** in the menu to see it again later.

## Where the art comes from

The artworks come live from the open-access collections of:
- the Art Institute of Chicago
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art
- the Cleveland Museum of Art
- the Victoria and Albert Museum
- Wikidata and Wikimedia Commons

If the site owner has added the free keys, results also come from Harvard Art Museums, Europeana and the Smithsonian. Every artwork links back to the museum that holds it.
